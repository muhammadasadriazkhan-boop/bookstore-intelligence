📚 I turned a website into a business dataset, and used it to answer "what should we sell?"

For my Business Data & Text Mining course, I built a Python web scraper for a practice bookstore site (books.toscrape.com). The site was the competitor and the scraper was my market-intelligence tool.

🔧 How it works
1️⃣ requests downloads each catalogue page
2️⃣ BeautifulSoup finds every product card and extracts the title, price, star rating, stock status and product link
3️⃣ The data gets cleaned: "£51.77" becomes 51.77, and star ratings hidden in CSS class names ("star-rating Three") become numbers 1–5
4️⃣ One reusable function runs inside a loop that follows the "next" button, so the same code handles 3 pages or the full catalogue
5️⃣ Pandas analyses the data and exports a clean CSV

📊 What 60 scraped books revealed
• Average price: £35.00
• 14 books are rated 5★, and they are the cheapest rating group on average (£32.48)
• The best-rated books start at just £15.94
• 100% of books are in stock, so a competitor can't win on availability alone

💷 The CEO challenge: maximise total star ratings with only £100
I treated it as a knapsack optimisation problem and used dynamic programming to choose 6 books for £96.59 with 26 total stars, the best possible score for that budget.

💡 My takeaway: rating quality doesn't track price. A new bookstore should lead with a "top-rated for less" range.
